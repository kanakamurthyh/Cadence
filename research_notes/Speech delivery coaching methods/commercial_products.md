# Commercial AI Speech-Delivery / Public-Speaking Coaching Products (state as of Oct 2026)

Researcher notes for Cadence (browser app comparing a user's reading of a fixed passage against several good human readings). Every claim carries a URL. Where only aggregator or review-site sources were found, that is stated. Products with thin evidence are listed under Gaps rather than padded.

## Q1. Which products exist, what do they measure, and what is their status?

### Takeaway
Two groups are commercially established. The first is general delivery coaches (Microsoft Speaker Coach, Yoodli, Orai, Speeko, LikeSo), which measure pace, fillers, pitch/monotone and some content features against generic norms. The second is reading or pronunciation coaches that work on a fixed text (Google Read Along, Microsoft Reading Progress/Coach, ELSA, BoldVoice), which align speech to known words and score each word or sound. None of the sources describe comparing a user's prosody against multiple reference human readings of the same passage. Poised shuts down on 8 Oct 2026, Ummo is gone from the App Store, and Microsoft's Teams Speaker Coach support article has been retired.

### Cited Findings

**Microsoft PowerPoint Speaker Coach (formerly Presenter Coach), active**
- Measures pacing, pitch (monotone), filler words, informal speech, euphemisms, culturally sensitive terms, wordiness, reading slides verbatim, repetitive language (iOS/Android first) and pronunciation (US English only) — [Microsoft Support: Rehearse with Speaker Coach](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/rehearse-your-slide-show-with-speaker-coach-cd7fc941-5c3b-498c-a225-83ef3f64f07b)
- Live on-screen nudges appear in the lower-right corner during rehearsal, and real-time feedback can be toggled on desktop/macOS. A Rehearsal Report follows the session, and users must screenshot it because it disappears when closed — [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/rehearse-your-slide-show-with-speaker-coach-cd7fc941-5c3b-498c-a225-83ef3f64f07b)
- Recommended pace is 100–165 WPM, "based on field study and past academic research", described as the rate most tested audiences find easiest to process. Pace is computed from "the most recent few seconds of speaking", and the report shows "a graph showing the rough variance of your speaking rate over time" — [Microsoft Support: Suggestions from Speaker Coach](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/powerpoint/suggestions-from-speaker-coach)
- Monotone guidance: "Speaking in monotone can make it harder for the audience to stay engaged"; it advises users to "emphasize the key words and phrases by adjusting your volume and pitch". No numeric pitch threshold is published — [Microsoft Support: Suggestions](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/powerpoint/suggestions-from-speaker-coach)
- Repetitive-language detection uses "a database of about 200 common words and phrases" and checks sentence starts (e.g. "basically"), endings (e.g. "right") and within sentences. Body-language feedback is available in PowerPoint for the web only — [Microsoft Support: Suggestions](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/powerpoint/suggestions-from-speaker-coach)
- Privacy: it runs in the cloud ("powered by Microsoft Speech Services. Your speech utterances will be sent to Microsoft") and needs an internet connection — [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/rehearse-your-slide-show-with-speaker-coach-cd7fc941-5c3b-498c-a225-83ef3f64f07b)

**Microsoft Teams Speaker Coach, status unclear**
- Gives private in-meeting feedback on pacing, pitch, filler words, informal speech, euphemisms and inclusiveness. Small private prompts appear during the meeting (e.g. "speaking too quickly"), and a post-meeting report is private and cannot be shared. It is enabled via More > Language and speech > Turn on Speaker Coach — [UT Dallas KB](https://atlas.utdallas.edu/TDClient/30/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=882); [Cornell IT](https://it.cornell.edu/teams/speaker-coach-helps-you-improve-your-presentations)
- Microsoft's own support article ("Improve your presenting skills with Teams meeting coach") now shows "The content of the article you were trying to reach is no longer available", which may mean the feature has been retired. I could not confirm this — [Microsoft Support (retired page)](https://support.microsoft.com/en-au/office/improve-your-presenting-skills-with-teams-meeting-coach-b9dad791-9f8e-4960-a029-38959cc50755)

**Yoodli, active**
- Analyzes filler words, pacing, eye contact (webcam), vocabulary diversity, talk-to-listen ratio and conciseness, and offers AI role-plays. These details come from review and aggregator sites only — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/1064550/yoodli); [Leadr review](https://leadr.co/?p=52594)
- Pace norm: "Average conversational pace is between 115 – 180 words per minute". It sends real-time alerts to slow down or speed up when the user leaves that range. The end-of-call report shows overall pacing plus a graph of variation, and the yoodli.ai dashboard tracks pacing over time — [Yoodli blog: slow down](https://yoodli.ai/blog/yoodli-weekly-tip-slow-down)
- Yoodli is a Toastmasters partner promoted inside Pathways. It tracks filler words, eye contact, pacing, word choice and body language, and the article reports reach in "149 countries" (Jan 2023) — [Yoodli blog: Toastmasters](https://yoodli.ai/blog/toastmasters-international-pathways-toward-success)

**Orai, active**
- Gives instant feedback on pace, filler words, energy, facial expression, conciseness, pausing and confidence. Its pace "sweet spot" is cited as 130–150 WPM. It was founded in 2017 in Philadelphia, and 2026 pricing is $9.99/month or $39.99/year. The pace and pricing figures come from aggregators — [Orai App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1203178170); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/orai-app-uses-ai-to-help-you-become-a-better-public-speaker); [beginnersinai](https://beginnersinai.org/orai-explained/)

**Poised, discontinuing**
- The official notice reads: "Poised is shutting down on 10/8/2026". It gave real-time in-meeting coaching visible only to the user, ran independently of the conferencing software, and provided live speaker notes — [poised.com](https://poised.com/)
- It measured pace, clarity, confidence, energy, empathy and filler words, and tracked progress privately — [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/ai-knowledge-base/tools/poised/)

**Speeko, active (iOS/macOS)**
- Measures filler words, pace, intonation, pausing, articulation, word choice and sentiment. It also offers bite-sized lessons, digital notecards, prompts and vocal warm-ups. Privacy claim: "No one has access to your recordings except you" — [Setapp](https://setapp.com/app-articles/speeko-public-speaking-coach); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/py/app/speeko-ai-for-public-speaking/id1071468459?l=en-GB); [speeko.co](https://speeko.co)

**Ummo, discontinued**
- Tracked fillers ("um", "uh", "like", "you know"), pace, "word power" and clarity. It is no longer on the App Store, although the website remains — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/ummo/about); [Paste review](https://pastemagazine.com/tech/app/ummo-app-review-ios-speech-therapy)

**LikeSo, status unverified**
- Gives a real-time "Articulate score" graded from A+ to "Uh Oh!", computed from the percentage of non-filler words over total words, plus pace — [Educational App Store](https://www.educationalappstore.com/best-apps/public-speaking-apps)

**VirtualSpeech (VR), active**
- A VR public-speaking practice app aimed at speaking anxiety. I found no sourced detail on its metrics in this pass — [Educational App Store](https://www.educationalappstore.com/best-apps/public-speaking-apps)

**Google Read Along (formerly Bolo in India), active; closest analogue to Cadence**
- Reading companion "Diya" listens as the child reads. Words turn blue once read correctly and a red underline flags mispronunciation. Diya steps in with help or encouragement when the child struggles — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/google-launches-a-new-read-along-app-to-help-you-with-homeschooling); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/05/07/google-launches-a-new-read-along-app-to-help-you-with-homeschooling)
- Privacy: speech is analyzed on the device in real time and "not synced, stored or analyzed on Google's servers" — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/05/07/google-launches-read-along-a-free-app-that-helps-young-children-practice-reading)
- It launched in India as Bolo and then expanded to 180 countries and 9 languages. A browser version followed in 2022 — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/05/07/google-launches-read-along-a-free-app-that-helps-young-children-practice-reading); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2022/08/09/google-read-along-website/)

**Microsoft Reading Progress and Reading Coach (Teams for Education / Immersive Reader), active**
- Students record a passage aloud, with Immersive Reader text settings available. Auto-detect then estimates errors and computes an accuracy rate and correct words per minute (CWPM). Error types are omission, insertion, mispronunciation, repetition and self-correction. No prosody or expression metric is listed — [Microsoft Support: Reading Progress](https://support.microsoft.com/topic/7617c11c-d685-4cb7-8b75-3917b297c407)
- Teachers can set pronunciation sensitivity from "Less sensitive" to "More sensitive", and can turn auto-detect off to suit learners' speech needs. It supports 80+ languages and dialects but "may not recognize all accents" well. Teachers choose whether students see a full report, a simplified report (color-coded errors only) or a custom one — [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/topic/7617c11c-d685-4cb7-8b75-3917b297c407); [Microsoft Education blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/education/blog/2023/02/enhance-literacy-development-with-reading-progress-and-coach/)
- Reading Coach practices the five most-missed words. The student listens to each word, sees it "stretched" (syllabified), views a picture, records themselves and gets instant pronunciation feedback, and progress is shown as stars and attempts — [Microsoft Education blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/education/blog/2023/02/enhance-literacy-development-with-reading-progress-and-coach/); [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/topic/7617c11c-d685-4cb7-8b75-3917b297c407)

**ELSA Speak, active**
- Grades pronunciation, fluency, intonation, word stress and listening. It covers 21 pronunciation skills plus syllable stress and gives instant feedback at syllable, word and sentence level. These details come from reviews and aggregators — [All Language Resources](https://www.alllanguageresources.com/elsa-speak/); [Zoftwarehub](https://zoftwarehub.com/en-ae/products/elsa-speak/features)

**BoldVoice, active**
- Short video lessons from Hollywood accent/dialect coaches, plus AI scoring of each sound in every word with tips. It covers sounds, intonation, rhythm and pitch, and paid 1:1 coaching is available — [BoldVoice FAQ](https://boldvoice.com/frequently-asked-questions); [BoldVoice accent coach](https://www.boldvoice.com/accent-coach); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/wellocution/id1567841142)

**Teleprompters with voice-following scroll, active**
- PromptSmart VoiceTrack (patented) scrolls as the user speaks, stops on pause or improvisation, and resumes when the user returns to the script. Speech recognition runs on-device with no internet needed. It supports many languages including Hindi — [Teleprompter.com vs PromptSmart](https://teleprompter.com/compare/teleprompter-vs-promptsmart); [PromptSmart Pro App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id894811756)
- BIGVU instead scrolls at a set WPM and adjusts speed line by line according to word count. This is a pacer, not voice-tracking — [BIGVU blog](https://bigvu.tv/blog/best-teleprompter-apps-481af/); [Kripesh Adwani review](https://kripeshadwani.com/bigvu-review/)
- Teleprompter.com also offers a voice-scrolling mode — [Teleprompter.com voice scrolling](https://www.teleprompter.com/features/voice-scrolling)

**Indian products**
- Stimuler (Bengaluru, founded 2022) runs speaking calls with an AI coach ("Sarah") across 100+ topics. It returns feedback on pronunciation, fluency, grammar, vocabulary, filler words, pace and clarity within roughly 20–30 s, and also covers IELTS prep — [Stimuler blog](https://stimuler.tech/blog/speak-vs-stimuler-2026); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1627501632)
- SpeakX (founded 2019) offers generative-AI conversation practice with feedback on pronunciation, grammar and vocabulary, using ASR trained on regional Indian accents. NITI Aayog's case study reports 10 million learners — [NITI Frontier Tech](https://frontiertech.niti.gov.in/story/ai-native-platform-enables-10-million-indians-to-learn-spoken-english-anytime-anywhere); [speakx.com](https://www.speakx.com/about)
- Enguru (Bengaluru, 2014) teaches functional English through gamified lessons and AI-simulated conversations, with instruction available from 28 languages. It focuses on content and English, not delivery metrics — [Dell Foundation](https://www.dell.org/story/english-learning-india-enguru/)
- Google Bolo was rebranded as Read Along (see above) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/05/07/google-launches-read-along-a-free-app-that-helps-young-children-practice-reading)

**Duolingo / Speak / Toastmasters / Alexa**
- Duolingo speaking exercises use speech recognition to give pronunciation feedback. I found only weak secondary sourcing for this — [idr.uin-antasari (thesis)](https://idr.uin-antasari.ac.id/28143/7/BAB%20IV.pdf)
- Toastmasters Pathways teaches more than 300 competencies. Its digital speech-feedback partner is Yoodli (see above) — [Yoodli blog](https://yoodli.ai/blog/toastmasters-international-pathways-toward-success)

### Inferences
- The UX pattern most relevant to Cadence is the fixed-text, word-aligned reader of Read Along and Reading Progress: highlight each word as it is read, mark problem words inline, then drill the missed words. PromptSmart's "pause when off-script, resume when back on" tracking is a proven way to follow a known passage live.
- Delivery coaches split into a live mode (subtle nudges in a screen corner, e.g. "slow down") and a post-session report (pace-over-time graph, filler counts). Cadence could do the same: give a minimal live cue during recording, then a per-phrase comparison view afterwards.
- The "listen to a model, then record" loop exists only at word level (Reading Coach plays the word and its stretched version; BoldVoice uses coach videos). Comparing phrase-level prosody against several human references looks like an open niche for Cadence. This is an inference from the absence of such a feature in the sources above, not a confirmed market fact.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm Teams Speaker Coach's current status. The support page is retired, but no retirement announcement was found.
- I found no primary sources for VirtualSpeech metrics, the Speak app's feedback mechanics, Duolingo's speaking scoring, any Toastmasters Alexa skill, or Teleprompter Premium's voice mode.
- LikeSo's current availability was not verified.
- I found no primary documentation of ELSA's scoring scale (e.g. the % per-sound scheme) or its "listen to native" UI details.

## Q2. What numbers do they use (WPM bands, filler thresholds), and are any validated?

### Takeaway
Published pace bands differ by product: Microsoft uses 100–165 WPM, Yoodli 115–180 (with a "typical" 130–170), and Orai 130–150. Only Microsoft cites field study and academic research as the basis, and even it publishes no validation data. No product discloses a numeric filler-rate or pitch-variance threshold.

### Cited Findings
- Microsoft: 100–165 WPM, computed over a rolling window of a few seconds and "based on field study and past academic research" — [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/powerpoint/suggestions-from-speaker-coach)
- Yoodli: "115 – 180 words per minute" average conversational pace, with live alerts outside that range — [Yoodli blog](https://yoodli.ai/blog/yoodli-weekly-tip-slow-down). A 130–170 "typical" range also appears in search snippets of Yoodli material.
- Orai: 130–150 WPM "sweet spot", from an aggregator — [beginnersinai](https://beginnersinai.org/orai-explained/)
- LikeSo: score = share of non-filler words, combined with pace (formula weights not published) — [Educational App Store](https://www.educationalappstore.com/best-apps/public-speaking-apps)
- BIGVU: a teleprompter pacer at a user-set WPM, adjusted per line by word count — [BIGVU blog](https://bigvu.tv/blog/best-teleprompter-apps-481af/)
- Reading Progress uses CWPM and accuracy %, the standard oral-reading-fluency measures, with adjustable pronunciation sensitivity — [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/topic/7617c11c-d685-4cb7-8b75-3917b297c407)

### Inferences
- Generic WPM bands are not appropriate for fixed-passage reading aloud. Cadence's approach of deriving the target from reference readings of the same passage is more defensible than any published band. If a band is shown at all, 100–165 is the only one with a stated research basis.
- Pitch/monotone feedback in commercial tools is qualitative (e.g. "emphasize key words by adjusting volume and pitch"), with no published thresholds.

### Gaps
- No product publishes a filler-per-minute threshold, a pitch-variance threshold, or validation of its scores against human raters.

## Q3. Is there published evidence of user outcomes?

### Takeaway
Read Along is the only product with a controlled outcome study I could find: an ASER-assisted pilot with a control group, plus an independent Sattva evaluation. The delivery coaches (Yoodli, Orai, Speaker Coach) have no published outcome studies in the sources found.

### Cited Findings
- Read Along / Bolo pilot ran Oct 2018 to Jan 2019 in 200 villages in Unnao, UP, with about 1,500 children in grades 1–5: 920 in the treatment group and 600 in the control. The ASER Centre ran Hindi assessments at baseline, 45 days and 90 days, and app use was unsupervised. 64% of treatment children improved at least one ASER level versus 40% of controls (p<0.0001). 39% versus 28% reached the top level (Std 2 text) at endline. About 92% of parents noticed improvement. Google itself notes the limited scale and an older app version — [Google Read Along pilot findings PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/read_along_pilot_findings.pdf)
- A Sattva Consulting independent assessment covered more than 3,500 students in 3 languages across 5 Indian states. It found that "40 percent more students" using Read Along improved one or more reading levels compared with non-users — [Sattva report](https://www.sattva.co.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Sattva_Google_Read-Along-IA-Report.pdf)
- Google's internal analysis reports that after 100 minutes on the app, beginner readers' oral reading fluency rose from 38% to 88%. This is unverified internal data — [readalong.google impact](https://readalong.google/impact/) (via search snippet)
- Small academic studies report gains: a 6-week study of Malaysian Year 5 ESL learners showed reading fluency and comprehension gains, and a 35-learner EFL study found speaking gains versus a control group — [ITLJ UTM](https://itlj.utm.my/index.php/itlj/article/view/282); [Academia (Ngo)](https://ntnu.academia.edu/ThuyNgo)

### Inferences
- The evidence supports word-level, real-time, read-aloud feedback for early readers. There is no evidence base yet for prosody-level delivery coaching, so Cadence should avoid outcome claims.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed outcome studies for Yoodli, Orai, Speeko, Poised, ELSA, BoldVoice or Microsoft Speaker Coach. Vendor testimonials were excluded.
