# Famous speech + multiple open readings: search notes (2026-10-14)

Goal: one famous recorded text (original by the famous speaker) + at least 3 other distinct human
readers of the same words, licence allowing modification (PD / CC0 / CC-BY / CC-BY-SA), for a 30-90 s
"good readers" envelope.

## Bottom line

No famous 20th-century *speech* (FDR Infamy, FDR 1933 inaugural, JFK inaugural, Eisenhower farewell,
Churchill) has more than ONE open human re-reading. LibriVox records these as single sections inside
Short Nonfiction / State of the Union / Farewell collections, never as multi-version projects.

The only option that meets the bar tonight is a famous *recited poem*:
**Tennyson reading "The Charge of the Light Brigade" (1890 wax cylinder, PD) + 17 LibriVox readers (PD).**

## Recommended: Tennyson, "The Charge of the Light Brigade"

Original (the famous speaker)
- https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/14/LightBrigade-Tennyson.ogg
- Commons page: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:LightBrigade-Tennyson.ogg
- Licence on Commons: "Public domain". Description: "1890 wax recording by Alfred Tennyson of his poem
  The Charge of the Light Brigade". 132 s, Vorbis 22.05 kHz.
- Quality: 1890 Edison cylinder. Heavy surface noise and band-limited; words partly hard to make out.
  Pitch tracking will be unreliable in places. Treat it as the "famous" reference, not as an envelope member.

Readers: LibriVox Fortnightly Poetry project, 1 Mar 2015, 17 recordings

- Licence: archive.org licenseurl = http://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/ (LibriVox: "All LibriVox
  recordings are in the public domain").
- Text source: https://www.bartleby.com/246/386.html (the standard final text, opening "Half a league, half a
  league, / Half a league onward"). All 17 are readings of the same text. Tennyson's recording is of the same
  final version. Check by ear for small slips before cutting the passage.
- Base URL: https://archive.org/download/charge_of_the_light_brigade_1503.poem_librivox/ (64 kb mp3; 128 kb

  It has no reader credit. It is a continuous archival noise floor with about 16 dB frame-energy spread and
  only one pause below -35 dB, so it is almost certainly the FDR archival recording, NOT another reader.
  I did not confirm this by listening.

Re-readings found:
- LibriVox Short Nonfiction Collection 064, section 4, reader Larry Wilson (lcw), PD mark:
  https://archive.org/download/nonfiction064_1904_librivox/snf064_dayofinfamy_roosevelt_lcw_64kb.mp3 (4:09).
  The text is Gutenberg #21805 ("Yesterday, December 7th, 1941 -- a date which will live in infamy --"), which
  matches the delivered opening.
- Nothing else. I searched LibriVox API title prefixes (Day, Pearl, Franklin, President, Address, Speech,
  Message, War, Joint, Inaugural), archive.org librivoxaudio full-text, a scan of every file title in 1,165
  LibriVox collection items (Short Nonfiction, Short Works, anniversary and other collections), archive.org
  audio with a licence, and Wikimedia Commons. Other archive.org uploads ("FDRInfamy", "DateOfInfamy",
  "FDR_Declares_War_19411208", "PearlHarborNarration-ADayOfInfamy") are the original audio or a narrated
  history, and/or have no licence.
- Related but different text: LibriVox Fireside Chats (id 11661), 9 Dec 1941 chat read by Larry Wilson. The
  original is PD too, but it is a different speech.

  recording of Lincoln exists.
- MLK: copyrighted, excluded.

## Method notes
- LibriVox API: `title=^Prefix` does prefix matching. Plain `title=` is close to exact. Collection sections are
  not searchable by the API, so I scanned the archive.org metadata of 1,165 LibriVox collection items for
  file titles (keywords inaug / infamy / roosevelt / kennedy / eisenhower / truman / churchill / fireside etc.).
- Scratch downloads (outside the repo): scratchpad/famous/gut21805/ and scratchpad/famous/tennyson/.
- I did not update `project context.md`, because the caller asked for no edits to project files.
