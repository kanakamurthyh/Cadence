# Historical recordings of prominent Indian speakers: availability and licence status (research notes, 2026-10-14)

Method note: Wikimedia Commons file metadata (licence templates, durations) was pulled directly from the Commons API, and Internet Archive metadata from the archive.org metadata API, on 2026-10-14. Durations come from the file metadata. Everything under "Inferences" is my legal reading, not legal advice.

## Q1. Nehru "Tryst with Destiny" (14-15 Aug 1947): is there an original recording, where is it hosted, and what is its licence status?

### Takeaway
Audio of the speech circulates on Wikimedia Commons (5:29) and the Internet Archive (4:40, labelled "Audio Source: All India Radio"). Both are shorter than the roughly 8-minute speech, so they are excerpts or edits. I found no reliable source that says whether this audio is the live midnight recording or a later re-recording; that question is still open. In India the recording is very likely public domain (60-year term under s.27). US status is uncertain, and the Commons licence rationale is technically wrong.

### Cited Findings
- Commons file "File:Tryst with Destiny- Speech by Pt. Jawaharlal Nehru.ogg": duration 329.3 s, Ogg. The source is given as "YouTube" (no URL), the date as 1947-08-14, and the licence template as `{{PD-old-70}}`, with no permission field. — [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tryst_with_Destiny-_Speech_by_Pt._Jawaharlal_Nehru.ogg)
- Internet Archive item `HindSwaraj-Speech-03-1`, "A Tryst with destiny", creator Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru, date 1947-08-14. Description: "Historical Speeches … Audio Source: All India Radio". The uploader (carl@media.org, the Public.Resource.Org "HindSwaraj"/"JaiGyan" collections) set licenseurl to CC0 1.0. Files are tryst.mp3 (4:40) and tryst.ogg (280.9 s). — [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/HindSwaraj-Speech-03-1)
- Internet Archive item `ATrystWithDestinyNehru1`, uploaded by an individual (creator "MEET"): 226 s, no licence declared, and no provenance. — [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/ATrystWithDestinyNehru1)
- Internet Archive item `dni.ncaa.ICCR-289-AC`, "Speech by Pandit Jawahar Lal Nehru". The source is the Indian Council for Cultural Relations (ICCR) via the National Cultural Audiovisual Archives (NCAA); side A runs 1283 s. The metadata does not say which speech it is, and no licence is declared. — [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/dni.ncaa.ICCR-289-AC)
- Before the English speech, Nehru spoke in Hindustani for about 6 minutes, followed by the roughly 8-minute English "Tryst with Destiny". The National Herald says "both the text and an audio recording survive, though they remain little known to the public", but it does not say whether the English audio is the live recording. — [National Herald](https://www.nationalheraldindia.com/national/jawaharlal-nehrus-forgotten-poetic-hindustani-prelude-to-tryst-with-destiny)
- The Wikipedia article and the University of Cambridge exhibition page both embed or link audio but give no provenance (live or re-recorded, which archive holds it). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tryst_with_Destiny); [Cambridge](https://www.cam.ac.uk/stories/tryst-with-destiny)
- Transcript: the full text is widely published, for example on American Rhetoric (where it was transcribed from audio) and in Janata Weekly. — [American Rhetoric](https://www.americanrhetoric.com/speeches/jawaharlalnehrutrystwithdestiny.htm); [Janata Weekly](https://janataweekly.org/articles/india-discovers-herself-again-the-full-text-of-jawaharlal-nehrus-tryst-with-destiny-speech)
- Indian Copyright Act s.27: copyright in a sound recording lasts "until sixty years from the beginning of the calendar year next following the year in which the sound recording is published". s.28 (Government works) uses the same 60-years-from-publication term. — [copyright.gov.in ch. V](https://copyright.gov.in/Copyright_Act_1957/chapter_v.html); [The Legal Lock](https://thelegallock.com/copyright-term-durations-in-india)
- Delhi HC, *Bignet Solutions LLP v. Novex Communications* (CS(COMM) 1094/2025, order of 10 Oct 2025) confirmed that sound recordings whose 60-year term has lapsed (for example pre-1965 film songs) are in the public domain and need no licence. — [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/who-owns-the-golden-age-delhi-hc-clarifies-no-licences-needed-for-pre-1965-bollywood-songs)

### Inferences
- India, recording: if the recording was published (broadcast or released) in 1947, its s.27/s.28 term ran out at the end of 2007. Even a re-recording issued as late as 1965 would be out of term by the end of 2025. So the AIR recording is very probably public domain in India, whether it was made live or later. One caveat: if AIR first "published" the audio much later (for example on an archival release after 1966), the term would run from that later date. This is uncertain.
- India, underlying text (the literary work): Nehru died on 27 May 1964. Under the life + 60 rule the text became public domain in India on 1 Jan 2025. It may also be a Government/Constituent Assembly work that is already public domain.
- Commons' `{{PD-old-70}}` tag is the wrong rationale. It is a life + 70 rule for authors, and on that basis Nehru's work would not be PD until 2035. The file is still probably PD in India for the s.27 reason above, but the Commons page itself does not establish that. US status is uncertain. From my background knowledge (not verified this session): a 1947 foreign recording still protected in India on 1 Jan 1996 could have had its US copyright restored under URAA, and the Music Modernization Act gives 1947-1956 recordings US protection until 2067. If that applies, US redistribution is a risk.
- The CC0 label on the archive.org copy is an assertion by the uploader (Public.Resource.Org). It is not a grant from Prasar Bharati or AIR, so it does not cure any rights AIR may still hold. Treat it as "probably PD in India", not as "CC0".
- The circulated clips (4:40 to 5:29) are shorter than the roughly 8-minute speech, so they are edited or excerpted. The audio quality is 1947 broadcast quality. That makes it a poor clean "good baseline" for acoustic analysis, but it works as a famous-speaker anchor. If the dataset needs many renditions of the same text, new readings of the public-domain text are the safer route.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative source (Prasar Bharati Archives, AIR, or a historian) confirming whether the circulated audio is the live 14-15 Aug 1947 recording or a later studio re-recording. The persistent claim that it is a re-recording could not be verified either way.
- I found no Prasar Bharati Archives terms of use. I did not locate the official AIR archive listing for this item.
- I did not verify which Nehru speech the ICCR/NCAA item contains.

## Q2. Gandhi "Spiritual Message" / "On God" (1931): copyright, hosting and Commons licence

### Takeaway
Two Commons copies exist (about 6 min 4 s each), both tagged public domain. The tag reasoning (PD-India + PD-1996) is strong: the recording's term in India expired long ago, which also blocks URAA restoration in the US. This is the best-cleared famous Indian speech recording I found.

### Cited Findings
- "File:Gandhi - His Spiritual Message to the World, 17 October 1931.mp3": 364.7 s. The source is archive.org item `mohandas-karamchand-gandhi-his-spiritual-message-to-the-world-17.10.1931`, and the licence template is `{{PD-old-70|Unclear-PD-US-old-70}}`. It is marked `{{Closed captions}}`, meaning a timed transcript exists on Commons, and is uploaded by Yann Forget. — [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gandhi_-_His_Spiritual_Message_to_the_World,_17_October_1931.mp3)
- "File:M. K. Gandhi's spiritual message.oga": 364.5 s. The source is GandhiServe (`streams.gandhiserve.org/.../augven_spiritual_message.wma`) and the author is given as "M. K. Gandhi / unknown recorder". The licence templates are `{{PD-India}}`, `{{PD-because|no copyright on sound recording in UK before 1957}}` and `{{PD-1996}}`. — [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:M._K._Gandhi%27s_spiritual_message.oga)
- Other 1931 Gandhi audio in [Category:Audio files of speeches by Mohandas K. Gandhi](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Audio_files_of_speeches_by_Mohandas_K._Gandhi), all with the same PD-India / PD-1996 style tags:
  - "Gandhi's public statement at London, 1931.oga" (18.3 s)
  - public message at Darwen, UK, 1931
  - speech while planting a tree, London, 3 Dec 1931
  - interview by US journalist
  - public message on satyagraha
  - IARC speech
  - a Hindi-speeches subcategory

  — [Commons file](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gandhi%27s_public_statement_at_London,_1931.oga)

### Inferences
- In India, a 1931 recording published in 1931 is well past any 60-year term. Gandhi died in 1948, so the text is also PD (life + 60 ended in 2008). Being PD in India on 1 Jan 1996 means the US copyright was not restored, which is the `{{PD-1996}}` logic. Derivatives and redistribution therefore look permissible in India and the US. Confidence: fairly high.
- The "no copyright on sound recording in UK before 1957" rationale may be imprecise. My recollection is that the UK 1911 Act protected records for 50 years, which would still have expired by about 1981. This does not change the conclusion.
- The Columbia Gramophone / Kingsley Hall attribution in the brief is not confirmed by these file pages, which give "unknown recorder" or the date 17 Oct 1931. Note this as unverified. The audio is noisy 1931 disc quality, and English is delivered in Gandhi's soft, slow style. It could anchor a "famous original" item, but it is a poor anchor for a clean baseline.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm the recording company or venue (Columbia, Kingsley Hall) from a primary source.
- The archive.org source item's metadata returned nothing (it may have been removed or darkened), so I could not check its licence label.

## Q3. Other candidates (Bose, Naidu, Tagore, Vivekananda, Ambedkar, AIR, Parliament/Sansad TV, GODL/data.gov.in, PIB, Mann Ki Baat)

### Takeaway
- **Bose:** has long (12-18 min) 1943 Tokyo-radio speeches on Commons tagged PD-Japan.
- **Tagore:** recordings exist (Berlin 1921 and Indian discs from the 1930s), but I found none on Commons. The Commons hits are modern pronunciations or recitations by others.
- **Vivekananda:** no recording survives.
- **Ambedkar:** a real 1955 BBC interview exists and is BBC copyright, so it is not usable. Viral "Ambedkar voice" clips are from a 2000 film.
- **Naidu:** I found no historical recording.
- **Government sources:** PIB-style "reproduce accurately, not in a misleading context" terms are not compatible with flaw injection. GODL-India does allow derivatives, but it covers datasets published on data.gov.in, and I found no speech audio under it. Sansad TV and Mann Ki Baat have no open licence; there is only attribution guidance.

### Cited Findings
- Bose, "File:Speech of Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose from Tokyo Radio, 1943.ogg": 1101.8 s, sourced from YouTube 8n9c9qdZoVI, licence `{{PD-Japan}}`. — [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Speech_of_Netaji_Subhas_Chandra_Bose_from_Tokyo_Radio,_1943.ogg)
- Bose, "File:Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose speech for the Consolidation of Greater East Asia.ogg": 706.4 s, dated 1943-11-08, sourced from YouTube vofeyJ_jnms, licence `{{PD-Japan}}`. — [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Netaji_Subhas_Chandra_Bose_speech_for_the_Consolidation_of_Greater_East_Asia.ogg)
- Bose, other files: "Speech of Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose Tokyo 1943.ogg", a Bengali "টোকিও বেতার বক্তৃতা" file, and "Subhas Chandra Bose name in his own voice.ogg". Their licences were not individually checked. — [Commons search](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?search=Subhas+Chandra+Bose+speech+filetype%3Aaudio&ns6=1)
- Tagore, Berlin 1921: the Humboldt-Universität Lautarchiv holds a shellac (catalogue Aut 48) of the closing words of "The Message of the Forest". It was recorded on 4 June 1921 at the Hotel Esplanade, Berlin, separately from the 1 June live lecture. The page states no reuse licence. — [Lautarchiv HU Berlin](https://www.lautarchiv.hu-berlin.de/en/collections-and-catalog/examples/famous-people-rabindranath-tagore/)
- Tagore, Indian discs: he cut nearly 20 records, including Hindusthan Records discs in 1932 (for example "Chotto Birpurusher Kahini"), and a private collector archive holds these. — [Millennium Post](https://www.millenniumpost.in/kolkata/kolkata-241090); [Learning & Creativity](https://learningandcreativity.com/?p=17270)
- Tagore on Commons: searches return only a 2.6 s modern pronunciation ("Hi-Rabindranath Tagore.ogg", PD-self) and modern readings of Tagore short stories (.oga). There is no file in Tagore's own voice. — [Commons file](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hi-Rabindranath_Tagore.ogg)
- Vivekananda: "there is no voice recording of Swami Vivekananda available to us today". There was no arrangement for recording at the 1893 Parliament of Religions, and the circulating "original voice" clips are fakes or film clips. Sources: a Ramakrishna Math/Mission fact-check, a Belur Math clarification, and BOOM. — [RKMM Awake](https://awake.rkmm.org/portal/en/community/topic/a-voice-recording-of-swami-vivekananda-addressing-sisters-and-brothers-of-america-is-popular-in-social-media-does-such-a-voice-recording-really-exist); [Belur Math](https://media.belurmath.org/clarification-on-swami-vivekanandas-voice-recording-swami-suvirananda-video-6719/); [BOOM](https://www.boomlive.in/movie-clipping-shared-as-rare-recording-of-vivekanandas-speech-in-chicago)
- Ambedkar: the authentic audio is a 1955 BBC radio interview in New Delhi, re-shared by the BBC in 2023. Viral "original voice" clips come from the 2000 film *Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar* (Mammootty). The Commons audio search for "Ambedkar speech" returned 0 results. — [Fact Crescendo](https://english.factcrescendo.com/viral-audio-misrepresents-dr-b-r-ambedkars-speech-original-voice-second-round-table-conference/); [Factly](https://factly.in/an-audio-clip-from-the-movie-dr-babasaheb-ambedkar-has-been-shared-as-the-original-voice-of-dr-ambedkar)
- Sarojini Naidu: the only Commons hits are a 2.5 s modern name pronunciation ("Sarojini Naidu.ogg", CC BY-SA 3.0, 2012) and a Hindi .wav name pronunciation. I found no historical speech audio. — [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sarojini_Naidu.ogg)
- PIB copyright policy: material "may be reproduced free of charge" without prior approval, but it "must be reproduced accurately and not used in a derogatory manner or in a misleading context", and the source must be acknowledged. Third-party material is excluded. — [PIB Copyright Policy](https://pib.gov.in/Content/102_2_Copyright-Policy.aspx)
- GODL-India: grants a "worldwide, royalty-free, non-exclusive license to use, adapt, publish (either in original, or in adapted and/or derivative forms), translate, display, add value, and create derivative works for all lawful commercial and non-commercial purposes", with mandatory attribution (provider, source, licence, DOI/URL). It was issued under NDSAP. — [OSM wiki summary](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Open_Geospatial_Data_from_Government_of_India); [Gazette notification](https://data.gov.in/sites/default/files/Gazette_Notification_OGDL.pdf)
- Sansad TV: an MIB advisory (reported Oct 2026) asks private channels to display "Courtesy: Sansad TV" when they use parliamentary footage or Sansad TV content. It is an attribution norm, not an open licence. — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/media-tv-news/mib-asks-private-tv-channels-to-prominently-credit-sansad-tv-for-parliamentary-footage-158975.html)
- Mann Ki Baat: audio files are posted per episode on News On AIR. Per the search results, private radio, TV and community radio "may use the broadcast free of cost from AIR". I found no CC or GODL licence. — [News On AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/mann-ki-baat-post/mann-ki-baat-114th-episode-audio-file/)
- AIR / NCAA archive audio on archive.org (Public.Resource.Org "JaiGyan"/"IndiaCulture" collections) generally carries no explicit licence, for example the ICCR Nehru item above. — [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/dni.ncaa.ICCR-289-AC)

### Inferences
- Bose 1943 Tokyo radio: in India, publication in 1943 means the s.27 term ended in 2003. Bose's own text rights are complicated by his uncertain date of death (1945, disputed). PD-Japan relies on Japan's old term for broadcast or recordings. US status is uncertain because the files are YouTube-sourced and the provenance is weak. These are usable at moderate risk, the audio quality is poor, and the content is political wartime propaganda.
- The PIB, PMIndia and GIGW-style terms ("reproduce accurately, not in a misleading context") are, in my reading, incompatible with deliberate PSOLA flaw injection. Commons generally does not treat GIGW terms as a free licence. I would not use PIB, Mann Ki Baat or Sansad TV audio for modified redistribution. They might be usable unmodified as reference with attribution, but that is still uncertain.
- GODL-India would be fine for derivatives, but I found no famous speech audio published on data.gov.in under GODL.
- Tagore: the 1921 Berlin and 1930s Indian discs are probably PD in India (well past 60 years), but no openly hosted copy with clear provenance was found. Using one would need sourcing from the Lautarchiv or a digitised disc, plus a provenance check. The Lautarchiv's own terms for digitised files are unknown.

### Gaps
- I did not check Sansad TV's or Prasar Bharati's formal terms-of-use pages directly.
- I could not confirm whether the Mann Ki Baat or PMIndia site copyright policy is identical to PIB's.
- I found no Sarojini Naidu speech recording. I did not search the British Library or BBC archives for one.
- Search found no early AIR broadcast audio under an open licence.

## Q4. Multiple openly licensed renditions of the same text (Preamble of the Constitution of India, "Tryst with Destiny")

### Takeaway
Openly licensed multi-speaker renditions are almost nonexistent. Commons has exactly one Preamble recitation (Malayalam translation, CC0, 49.8 s), and the Internet Archive search returned no Indian Preamble recitations. For "Tryst with Destiny", the archive.org copies are Nehru's own audio, not readings by other people. A multi-reader baseline would have to be newly recorded, with readers releasing their audio under CC0 or CC-BY.

### Cited Findings
- "File:ഭരണഘടനയുടെ ആമുഖം - Preamble to the Constitution of India Read in Malayalam.ogg": 49.8 s, recorded 2024-03-05 by User:Asdofindia. It is a reading of the official Malayalam translation by the Legislative Department, licensed `{{self|cc-zero}}`. It is the only file in [Category:Preamble to the Constitution of India](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Preamble_to_the_Constitution_of_India), and the only Commons audio hit for "Preamble India". — [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%E0%B4%AD%E0%B4%B0%E0%B4%A3%E0%B4%98%E0%B4%9F%E0%B4%A8%E0%B4%AF%E0%B5%81%E0%B4%9F%E0%B5%86_%E0%B4%86%E0%B4%AE%E0%B5%81%E0%B4%96%E0%B4%82_-_Preamble_to_the_Constitution_of_India_Read_in_Malayalam.ogg)
- Internet Archive audio search for "preamble AND (india OR constitution)" returned only US Constitution and podcast items, with nothing for India's Preamble. For "tryst with destiny" it returned the three Nehru-voice items listed in Q1 and no third-party readings under an open licence. — [archive.org advanced search](https://archive.org/advancedsearch.php?q=preamble+AND+%28india+OR+constitution%29+AND+mediatype%3Aaudio)
- Commons audio search for "Tryst with Destiny" returned only the single Nehru file. — [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tryst_with_Destiny-_Speech_by_Pt._Jawaharlal_Nehru.ogg)
- LibriVox has a public-domain read of the US Preamble ("PreambleToTheConstitutionOfTheUnitedStates", CC BY 3.0 US). This shows the multi-reader format exists for the US text, but not for India's. — [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/PreambleToTheConstitutionOfTheUnitedStates)

### Inferences
- The Preamble text is a strong choice for the shared text. It is short (about 85 words in English), every Indian reader recognises it, and it is recited at mass events. As an enactment it can be reproduced under s.52(1)(q), and the 1950 text is likely a public-domain Government work in any case. Openly licensed readings, however, would have to be collected by the project, for example through Lingua Libre or its own CC0 recording drive.
- "Tryst with Destiny" works as a single famous anchor, with Nehru's audio probably PD in India. Third-party readings would also have to be commissioned. The English text is likely free to use in India from 2025 onward (life + 60 after Nehru's 1964 death).

### Gaps
- I did not search Lingua Libre or YouTube CC-BY filters for Preamble recitations. YouTube CC-BY uploads may exist but would need item-by-item verification.
- Other Commons language categories (Hindi, Tamil and so on) might hold uncategorised Preamble readings that this search did not catch.
