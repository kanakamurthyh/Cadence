# Cadence's multi-reader comparison fills an open niche

Speech coaching products fall into two groups. Delivery coaches such as Microsoft Speaker Coach, Yoodli, Orai and Speeko score pace, fillers and monotone against generic norms. Fixed-text reading coaches such as Google Read Along, Microsoft Reading Progress, Azure Pronunciation Assessment and Amira align speech to a known passage and flag individual words. **None of the sources describe a product that compares a user's prosody against several human readings of the same passage.** The research precedent for that idea is strong. Project LISTEN scored children by correlating their word-level contours with an adult narration and predicted posttest fluency at r = 0.872. The Schwanenflugel lab found that matching an adult's pitch contour, together with avoiding mid-sentence pauses, predicts reading skill. The pedagogy also favours the two modes Cadence already has: assisted repeated reading has the best evidence base (Therrien ES = 0.75 for fluency), and Read Naturally's "cold timing, then practise with a model, then hot timing" loop has the same shape as Test → Practice → Test. For tonight, the highest-value moves are mostly UI framing on data Cadence already computes:

- a cold-versus-hot before/after score;
- "you vs. closest reader" A/B playback of a flagged span;
- relabelling pause flags with the validated "intrusion vs. missing break" vocabulary;
- a four-dimension Test-mode breakdown taken from the MDFS rubric.

Live voice-tracking and new acoustic models are high-effort and can wait.

## The market splits between generic pace bands and word-level reading tutors

Delivery coaches run a live mode plus a report. During rehearsal, PowerPoint Speaker Coach shows small nudges in a screen corner. Afterwards it produces a Rehearsal Report with "a graph showing the rough variance of your speaking rate over time". Its pace is computed from "the most recent few seconds of speaking" and judged against **100–165 WPM**, which it says is "based on field study and past academic research" ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/powerpoint/suggestions-from-speaker-coach)). Other products use different bands. Yoodli alerts outside **115–180 WPM** ([Yoodli](https://yoodli.ai/blog/yoodli-weekly-tip-slow-down)), and Orai's "sweet spot" is reportedly **130–150 WPM** ([beginnersinai](https://beginnersinai.org/orai-explained/)). Monotone feedback is purely qualitative everywhere: Microsoft only tells users to "emphasize the key words and phrases by adjusting your volume and pitch". **No vendor publishes a filler or pitch threshold, or any validation against human raters.** The category is also unstable. Poised announced it is "shutting down on 10/8/2026" ([poised.com](https://poised.com/)), and Microsoft's Teams Speaker Coach support page now returns "no longer available" ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-au/office/improve-your-presenting-skills-with-teams-meeting-coach-b9dad791-9f8e-4960-a029-38959cc50755)).

Fixed-text reading coaches are much closer to Cadence. In Google Read Along, words turn blue as they are read correctly and a red underline flags mispronunciation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/05/07/google-launches-a-new-read-along-app-to-help-you-with-homeschooling)). Its team deliberately "optimized for recall, thereby increasing the number of false positives, which results in a user experience that feels more encouraging" ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2020/06/on-device-ML-design-insights.html)). It is also the only product with a controlled outcome study. In the pilot, **64% of treatment children improved at least one ASER level versus 40% of controls (p<0.0001)** ([Google pilot PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/read_along_pilot_findings.pdf)). Microsoft Reading Progress reports accuracy and correct words per minute, tags omissions, insertions, mispronunciations, repetitions and self-corrections, and lets teachers adjust pronunciation sensitivity. It documents **no prosody metric**. Its companion Reading Coach drills the five most-missed words ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/topic/7617c11c-d685-4cb7-8b75-3917b297c407)). Azure Pronunciation Assessment is the most detailed commercial API. It tags each word as `UnexpectedBreak`, `MissingBreak` or `Monotone`, and Microsoft suggests a confidence threshold of 0.75 that you tune yourself. Its overall PronScore gives the most weight to the weakest dimension (reading with prosody: 0.4·lowest + 0.2·each of the others) ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/how-to-pronunciation-assessment)). Teleprompters show two ways to pace a reader. PromptSmart VoiceTrack follows the voice, pausing when the speaker goes off-script and resuming when they return ([SpeechTechMag](https://www.speechtechmag.com/Articles/News/Speech-Technology-News/PromptSmart-Launches-Teleprompter-App-with-Voice-Recognition---98763.aspx)). BIGVU simply scrolls at a set WPM, adjusted line by line ([BIGVU](https://bigvu.tv/blog/best-teleprompter-apps-481af/)).

Taken together, Cadence's reference-derived pace target is more defensible than any published WPM band, because the bands disagree with each other and none was built for reading a fixed passage aloud. The "model reading" loop exists commercially only at word level: Reading Coach plays each word and a stretched version of it, and BoldVoice uses coach videos. Comparing a user's phrase-level prosody against several readers is the gap. This conclusion rests on not finding such a feature, so it is an inference rather than a verified market fact.

## Research has already validated "compare against adult readings"

Cadence's consensus envelope is a multi-reader version of Project LISTEN's template model. LISTEN measured each word's latency, duration, mean F0 and intensity, then scored a child by the Pearson correlation between the child's contour and an adult narration of the same sentence. Correlation was chosen because it factors out "differences between adults and children in baseline F0 … individual variations in intensity, and fluctuations in recording conditions". Combining correlation and latency features predicted posttest fluency at **r = 0.872**, or **0.965** when the pretest was added, against 0.809 for the pretest alone ([Mostow & Duong 2009](https://www.scs.cmu.edu/afs/cs.cmu.edu/Web/People/listen2/pdfs/AIED2009-expressiveness-final.pdf)). The same paper adds a warning that matters for Cadence's UI. Human agreement on single sentences was only **kappa 0.087–0.297**, and the authors conclude "a single sentence is insufficient to rate reliably." Per-word flags should therefore be presented as hints, and verdicts should be aggregated over the whole passage. A later LISTEN study found that generalised models trained on 11 adult narrators beat single-adult templates ([Duong, Mostow & Sitaram 2011](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/two-methods-for-assessing-oral-reading-prosody)), which supports Cadence's use of several readers over one.

The Schwanenflugel lab's acoustic measures give Cadence a validated feedback vocabulary. Three features predicted reading accuracy and comprehension, and pitch contour predicted later fluency even after word-reading speed was controlled ([PMC2805245](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2805245)):

- **Pause intrusions**: pauses inside sentences, not at punctuation.
- **Comma-pause duration**.
- **Child–adult pitch-contour match**.

Perception research supplies a sanity band. Listeners rated pauses of about **0.6 s** most natural, or a **1:2 comma-to-period ratio** (0.3/0.6 s or 0.6/1.2 s), and longer pauses made speech sound slower ([Liu et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8874014)). Speechace's API uses similar bands, treating pauses of 500 ms or more as "medium" and over 1 s as "long" ([Speechace](https://api-docs.speechace.com/api-reference/score-text-fluency/handling-fluency-response)). For monotony, the speaker-normalised standard is the semitone SD of F0 ([arXiv 1801.00453](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.00453)). One patent flags speech as monotone when the pitch variation quotient falls below 0.16 ([US11205418](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11205418)). The newest work supports Cadence's multi-reference design directly: DTW over WavLM features, scored against every available native template and averaged, "exceeds human agreement" on holistic scoring, while intonation scoring remains "more modest" ([McIntosh et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13721)).

## Pedagogy endorses Practice-then-Test, but guidance should fade

Repeated oral reading with a model and feedback is the best-supported fluency method. The National Reading Panel found a weighted **ES of 0.41** across guided oral reading studies ([NRP ch.3](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/sites/default/files/publications/pubs/nrp/Documents/ch3.pdf)), and Therrien's meta-analysis reports **0.75 for fluency and 0.73 for comprehension** ([ASHA summary](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/903ec865-92fd-4ca5-9cea-8534b161420a)). Assisted approaches, where the learner reads with or after a model, beat unassisted ones ([PMC2783645](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2783645)). The typical dose is **3–5 rereadings** of one passage ([BRT NRP update](https://brtprojects.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Fluency-NRP-Updates-TRL-2020.pdf)). Read Naturally packages this into a cycle:

1. An unpracticed one-minute **cold timing** sets the baseline.
2. The student reads along with an audio model, typically three times.
3. The student practises alone, three to ten times, until reaching the goal.
4. A **hot timing** is scored, including an expression rating, and shown next to the cold score as before-and-after feedback ([Read Naturally](https://readnaturally.com/research/read-naturally-strategy); [knowledge base](https://readnaturally.com/knowledgebase/q-and-a/40)).

Cadence's Test → Practice → Test already follows this structure. What it lacks is the side-by-side before/after display.

The case for a pacer is weaker than it looks. The Reading Plus Guided Window reported about a 1 SD effect, but the study covered *silent* reading and is published by the vendor ([Reading Plus](https://www.readingplus.com/efficacy/exploring-a-guided-silent-reading-intervention/)). Motor-learning research also warns against permanent scaffolds. The guidance hypothesis holds that constant concurrent feedback creates dependence. A pre-registered meta-analysis found that reducing feedback frequency gave only **g = 0.21 at delayed retention**, which was not significant ([McKay et al. 2021](https://www.scapps.org/jems/index.php/1/article/view/2529)). Fading the pacer across repetitions is therefore a reasonable default, not a proven win. Practice-mode scores should not be presented as evidence of learning; only unguided Test mode should be.

Two techniques from second-language learning fit Practice mode. In shadowing, the learner repeats a model's speech almost simultaneously; it improved comprehensibility and fluency but not accentedness ([Foote & McDonough 2017](https://jalt-publications.org/sites/default/files/pdf-article/45.6tlt-art3.pdf)), which suits a delivery coach. A real-time pitch display helped learners of French intonation, and the gains generalised to novel sentences ([Hardison 2004](https://doaj.org/article/581055d4d4ec401da83bbc04117ec8c9)). The research does not agree on everything. Kuhn's wide-reading condition, which used three texts a week instead of one, matched repeated reading and showed gains earlier. That argues for rotating passages once the user reaches a criterion, rather than drilling one passage indefinitely.

## Ranked build list for tonight: framing beats new models

The table ranks additions by impact relative to effort, assuming Cadence already has these pieces:

- wav2vec2 word alignment and the per-word consensus envelope;
- pause marks and a pace gauge;
- per-session storage of the weak-spot tip.

Effort estimates are rough hours for one developer.

| Rank | Addition | Precedent | Impact | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cold vs. hot card**: store the first Test score per passage and show it beside the latest Test score after Practice | Read Naturally before/after graph | High (main motivational device) | ~0.5 h |
| 2 | **A/B playback**: on a flagged span, play the user's slice, then the same span from the closest reference reader, using word timestamps | LISTEN adult narrations; no vendor documents this | High (the visible differentiator) | ~1–1.5 h |
| 3 | **Rename pause flags**: "pause inside a phrase" (intrusion / UnexpectedBreak) vs. "no pause at comma/period" (MissingBreak), with a 0.3–0.6 s / 0.6–1.2 s sanity band | Azure taxonomy; Schwanenflugel; Liu 2022 | High (validated, easy to explain) | ~0.5–1 h |
| 4 | **Four-dimension Test breakdown**: expression, phrasing, smoothness, pace mapped from envelope flags, with an overall score weighted towards the weakest dimension | MDFS rubric; Azure PronScore | Medium-high | ~1 h |
| 5 | **"Listen first" step in Practice**: play a reference reading with the pacer before the user reads (assisted reading), then the user reads with the pacer | Kuhn & Stahl; Read Naturally step 2 | Medium-high (strongest pedagogy) | ~0.5–1 h if reference audio is served |
| 6 | **Fade the pacer**: full phrase highlight on rep 1, phrase-boundary ticks on rep 2, off on rep 3, then suggest Test | Guidance hypothesis; McKay 2021 | Medium | ~0.5 h |
| 7 | **Lenient flagging copy**: frame flags as hints, show at most the top 2–3 per read, and judge at passage level | Read Along recall bias; LISTEN kappa < 0.3 | Medium (protects the demo from false-positive embarrassment) | ~0.5 h |
| 8 | **Pace gauge labelled by readers**: "readers' range" band from references, with 100–165 WPM only as a fallback caption | Microsoft band is the only research-backed one | Low-medium | ~0.25 h |
| 9 | **Per-sentence contour correlation** (pitch, duration) as a baseline-robust "expression match" | LISTEN template model | Medium | ~1–2 h |
| 10 | **Pitch overlay after each attempt**: the user's F0 over the reader band | de Bot; Hardison | Medium | ~2 h |
| 11 | **Semitone-SD monotony flag** per phrase vs. the reader range | arXiv 1801.00453; PVQ patent | Medium | ~1 h plus threshold fitting |
| 12 | **Live voice-tracking cursor** (pointer moves only forward, pauses when the reader goes off-script) | PromptSmart VoiceTrack; Read Along | High in polish | 4 h+, risky; skip tonight |
| 13 | **External validation** of envelope-violation rate against speechocean762 prosody labels | GOPT benchmarks | High for credibility | Days; post-hackathon |

Items 1–4 and 7–8 make the core evidence-backed story visible and fit together in about four hours. Item 2 is the one most likely to make judges remember the product. Any new threshold, such as the semitone-SD cutoff or the pause bands used as hard flags, must be fitted on T1 only and never tuned on T2 or T3. Items 9–11 belong after the core is solid. Cadence should make **no outcome claims**. Read Along is the only coach with controlled evidence, and that evidence covers word-level reading for children, not prosody coaching for adults.

## Conclusion

Cadence's novelty is not in measuring prosody, since Azure, ETS and LISTEN already do that. It is in **explaining a delivery gap by playing back how real people read the same words**. No commercial product documents that. The research suggests the explanation will be most credible when it follows three habits: use the validated vocabulary of pause intrusions, missing breaks and contour match; aggregate verdicts over the passage instead of judging single words; and keep the scored Test unguided. The open risk is that almost all of the fluency evidence comes from children aged 5–18, so it is not established that repeated assisted reading improves adult delivery. Each cold-to-hot pair Cadence records is a small dataset that could test that question.
